## Day 36 — Cognitive Pattern Explorer

### What I Built
A single-file HTML app (vanilla JavaScript only, no frameworks) offering a
calm, game-like self-reflection experience around thinking patterns —
explicitly educational, using reflective language ("you often...", "this
suggests...") rather than clinical labels or diagnosis.

### Thinking Tendencies Measured
Analytical Thinker, Emotional Intuitive, Overthinking Loop Style,
Action-First Decision Maker, Balanced Reflective Thinker.

### Flow
1. Start Screen — Calm Mode or Stress-Relief Mode
2. Chapter 1 — Discover Your Thinking Style (scenario responses)
3. Chapter 2 — Choose Your Priorities (draggable cards)
4. Chapter 3 — Map Your Thinking (draggable timeline)
5. Final Reflection Journal — personalized insights + percentage breakdown

### My Results (Calm Mode)
| Tendency | Share |
|---|---|
| Overthinking Loop Style (leading pattern) | 30% |
| Emotional Intuitive | 27% |
| Analytical Thinker | 23% |
| Balanced Reflective Thinker | 13% |
| Action-First Decision Maker | 7% |

**Sample scenario:** "You're choosing between two job offers. What matters
most in the first hour of thinking about it?" — I chose "which one feels
right when you imagine yourself there" over the more analytical options.

**Journal reflection given:** "You often revisit a decision from many
angles, which can surface useful nuance but may also loop back on itself
before settling." Paired with Emotional Intuitive as the secondary pattern:
"You often lead with how something feels, reading emotional undercurrents
quickly and trusting your gut sense of a situation."

### What I Learned
My overthinking pattern is not disconnected from my intuition — it happens
right after it. I lead with a gut read (Emotional Intuitive at 27%), then
immediately run an analytical pass to double-check that instinct instead of
trusting it, which is exactly what produces the looping behavior scored
highest overall (30%). The app's reflective phrasing made the pattern
specific enough to actually notice, rather than something vague I could
wave off as "I overthink sometimes."
