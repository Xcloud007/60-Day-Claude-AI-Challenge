## Day 45 — AI Decision Strategist

### What I Built
A single self-contained interactive HTML Decision Report, generated after
a 4-question interview (the decision and options, goal/timeline, gut
instinct vs. hesitation, biggest fear and reversibility). Built strictly
from my own answers, with a decisive verdict rather than hedged advice.

### The Decision
**Option A — Skills-first** vs. **Option B — Project-first**, under a hard
3-month deadline, with a stated goal of both hiring and skill-building.

### Decision Matrix (out of 70)
| Dimension | Skills-first (A) | Project-first (B) |
|---|---|---|
| Life/Career Upside | 6 | 8 |
| Financial Safety | 7 | 7 |
| Growth & Learning | 6 | 8 |
| Stress Level (10=low) | 5 | 5 |
| Reversibility (10=easy) | 6 | 7 |
| Long-term Alignment | 5 | 7 |
| Regret Risk (10=low) | 3 | 5 |
| **Total** | **38** | **47** |

### The Verdict
**Option B — Project-first wins.** It wins because I have a hard 3-month
deadline and a stated goal of both hiring and skill-building — only a
visible artifact moves both. My gut's "skills-first" instinct was called
out as fear of judgment, not a real strategic reason.

**What could flip it:** if the 7-day test shows I can't even scope a small
experiment, that's real evidence I need structured basics first.

**Hard truth:** "Enough skills first" is a finish line you get to keep
moving — a shipped project is not.

### Shareable Card Summary
Matrix Summary: Skills-first 38 | Project-first 47
Verdict: Project-first wins — "A shipped project beats a moving finish
line."
LinkedIn Hook generated: "I almost let 'not ready yet' cost me 3 months."

### What I Learned
The Decision Matrix scores weren't actually where the value was — Project-
first only won by 9 points, close enough that a spreadsheet alone wouldn't
have settled it. The real value was the Verdict naming what the score
couldn't: that my "skills-first" preference was fear of being judged
before I'm ready, not a genuine strategic read of the situation. Once that
was named directly, the close numeric gap stopped mattering — the decision
was never really about which option scored higher, it was about which
reason for choosing was real.
