# My Claude Usage Strategy

**Profile:** Student · Daily user · Focus on Learning, Research, Content Creation · Priority: Learning Support
*(Also factoring in your data science learning path and the 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge you're working through)*

---

## Recommended Primary Model
**Claude Sonnet 5**

## Why This Model Fits Me
- You use Claude **daily**, so you need a model that's fast enough not to slow you down, but smart enough to actually teach and explain — not just answer.
- Your work (learning Python/SQL/stats, researching stock fundamentals, building the Football Intelligence Hub) needs real reasoning, not just quick lookups.
- Sonnet 5 balances quality and speed well, and is cost-efficient enough for heavy daily use without hitting limits fast.
- Opus is overkill for most learning/research tasks; Haiku is too shallow for concept explanations.

---

## When to Use Haiku
- Quick fact checks, definitions, syntax reminders ("what does `.groupby()` do?")
- Fast rewrites/formatting of text
- Simple summarization of short content
- When you're on a time crunch and just need a quick nudge, not a full explanation

## When to Use Sonnet
- **Your default for almost everything**: learning new concepts (stats, ML, Python), explaining code, structuring research, drafting content
- Building your Claude Skills (stock research, challenge modules)
- Debugging code and getting step-by-step explanations
- Day-to-day football/stock research and analysis

## When to Use Opus
- Complex multi-step reasoning: e.g., designing a full data science roadmap, comparing multiple ML approaches, deep business/strategy analysis
- Long, high-stakes research synthesis (e.g., a deep dive comparing 10+ stocks' fundamentals)
- When Sonnet's answer feels shallow or you're stuck on a genuinely hard problem

---

## Recommended Effort Level

### Low
Quick lookups, formatting, simple Q&A — pair with Haiku or Sonnet.

### Standard
**Your everyday setting.** Most learning explanations, coding help, content drafts, GitHub challenge tasks. Good balance of depth and speed.

### High
Use when: learning a genuinely new/hard topic (e.g., first time doing XGBoost or hypothesis testing), building a Claude Skill from scratch, or writing content you'll actually publish/submit.

### Max
Reserve for: complex research synthesis (comparing many data sources), designing your full data science career roadmap, or debugging a gnarly multi-file coding issue.

## My Personalized Claude Workflow

| Task | Best Model | Best Effort | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learning new DS/Python/stats concept | Sonnet | Standard–High | Needs clear teaching, not just facts |
| Debugging code | Sonnet | Standard | Fast iterative back-and-forth |
| Building Claude Skills (stock research) | Sonnet | High | Structured, reusable logic — worth the extra care |
| Quick syntax/fact check | Haiku | Low | Speed > depth |
| Football Intelligence Hub research | Sonnet | Standard | Multi-part but not extremely complex |
| Deep stock fundamental comparison | Opus | High–Max | Needs careful multi-factor reasoning |
| Content drafts (blog/notes/GitHub docs) | Sonnet | Standard | Good writing quality, fast turnaround |
| Career roadmap / big-picture planning | Opus | Max | High-stakes, multi-step strategic reasoning |
| Daily quick Q&A | Haiku or Sonnet | Low–Standard | Efficiency matters most |

---

## Biggest Mistakes I Should Avoid
1. **Using Opus for everyday learning questions** — wastes time/cost when Sonnet does the job well.
2. **Using Haiku for concept explanations** — you'll get shallow answers that don't actually build understanding.
3. **Always defaulting to Low effort** — for genuinely new topics, this shortchanges your learning; bump to High.
4. **Not saving/organizing outputs** — you already do this well (HTML files per stock/task) — keep it up, it makes review and reuse much easier.
5. **Treating every research task the same** — a quick stock check and a deep fundamentals comparison need different effort levels; don't over- or under-invest.

---

## Final Recommendation

If you could only pick **ONE model + ONE effort level** for most of your work:

**→ Claude Sonnet 5 at Standard effort**

**Why:** As a daily student user focused on learning, research, and content creation, you need consistent quality explanations without excessive wait times or cost. Sonnet at Standard effort handles 80%+ of your workload — coding help, concept learning, content drafts, skill-building — reliably well. Just remember to **bump to High effort** when tackling a genuinely new/hard topic, and **switch to Opus** only for the rare deep-research or big-picture strategy tasks.
