## Day 44 — LinkedIn Profile Optimizer

### What I Built
A single self-contained HTML app styled as a "Field Report" that audits a
real LinkedIn profile section by section, rebuilds it with the strategy
behind each change explained, and produces a before/after scorecard plus a
7-day activation plan. Built strictly from user-provided information —
no fabricated achievements or metrics.

### My Setup
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Subject | Rupesh Sharma |
| Goal | Network Growth |
| Auditor Persona | Ex-Recruiter, 50K+ profiles reviewed |

### Part 1 — The Roast
**Overall Profile Strength (Before): 31/100**

### Part 2 — The Rebuild
**Headline — 3 options generated:**
1. Keyword-optimized: "AI/ML Student (B.Tech) | Python & Data Analysis | Cloud Computing | Building Real-World AI Projects"
2. Value-proposition: "Helping teams turn AI ideas into working prototypes | B.Tech AI/ML Student | Python · Cloud · Data"
3. Authority style: "Documenting my journey from Design → Content → AI/ML | B.Tech Student building in public"

**Recommendation given:** for a Network Growth goal, lead with Option 2 or
3 — value-prop and authority headlines make people want to connect and
follow, while keyword headlines are built for recruiters actively
searching, which wasn't the current priority.

**About section hook (rewritten):** "I went from designing posters to
training models — and I'm documenting every step in between."

### Part 5 — What I Learned (Recap Card)
**Top 3 mistakes identified:**
1. About section opened with "passionate about technology" — the most generic line possible
2. Skills section had only 4 entries out of 50 available slots
3. Experience bullets described responsibilities, not results

**The #1 thing that made the biggest difference:** adding a real hook to
the first two lines of the About section — that's the only part visible
before someone clicks "see more."

### What I Learned
The single lowest-scoring, highest-leverage fix wasn't a rewrite of
substance — it was the two lines everyone actually sees before deciding
whether to read further. A profile can have real projects and skills
behind it and still fail to convert a viewer into a connection if the
visible hook does no work. The skills-section gap (4 of 50 slots used)
was the most mechanical, "just do it" fix — most of the score recovery
came from filling in unused surface area, not deep rewriting.
